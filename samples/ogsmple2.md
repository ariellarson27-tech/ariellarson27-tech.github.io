Gift
2012

The sound of car doors slamming woke me up. I was sprawled on the futon, the comforter wrapped around my middle like a human sausage roll. When the banging on the front doors made Desmond’s parents stir we both jumped up, hovering at the top of the stairs like we had for years as his dad answered the door. It was my mom. “Where is she?”

“What’s going on Annabelle?” His mom was somewhere to our right, on the bottom stair of the flight leading to the second floor.

“Evelynn needs to come with me, now, we can’t stay here.” She sounded scared.

“Why, what’s happened?” Desmond’s dad blocked her from coming in.

“I can’t – _tell_ you, it’s dangerous. We have to leave, _now!_”

Desmond slipped by me before I could bolt through the door. “Anne, you need to take a deep breathe, and start from the beginning. Did something happen after Evy left last night?” I could barely see my mom’s face through the crack, she was horrified of something. “She was almost snatched last night, right here off the doorstep. The guy was heading back towards your house.”

She shook her head violently. “You can’t know, they’ll get you too–”

“Where’s my dad?” I burst out the door, staying just behind Desmond.

She tried to grab me but Desmond blocked her, “Eve, we have to go. It’s too dangerous here.”

I shook my head, “I’m not going anywhere till you tell me where Dad is!”

Annabelle started to shake, holding in the sobs that were making her blur. “He’s… gone.”

I stared at her, numb to the words she said. “No, he was going to talk to you last night, let you go, so we would all be happy again. He’s–” Desmond took my hand and we ran passed my mom, bolting for my house. Once we were within sight of it I could see that the windows were smashed in, the door hanging crooked off its bottom hinge.

Before I could dash in there, terror in my throat, Desmond wrapped his arms around me tight and pulled out his phone from behind me. The sound of him tapping out nine-one-one rang in my ears as I stared at the house. “Nine-one-one, what is your emergency?”

“There’s been a robbery at… Jace Stanton is still unaccounted for… We’ll wait for them to arrive- Evie no!” I couldn’t stand it any longer, ducking out of his arms I darted into the house, careful not to touch anything.

“Gotta find gloves,” I muttered, stepping over the jackets and shoes strewn across the front hall to a pair of those cheap finger gloves that had been thrown against the stairs that lead to the second floor. Looking back I saw Desmond slipping through the door as well, pulling his sleeves over his hands to keep from touching anything. I signed ‘kitchen’ to him and put a finger to my lips before sliding further down the hall, bracing myself against the wall as Desmond passed me to get to the kitchen while I peeked into the living room.

I held my breath, the grandfather clock was smashed across the entry, its glass door and faerie figurines shattered and tangled in the chains and pendulum, splinters of cherry wood threatening to poke my thighs as I eased over it and crouched by an upturned couch. I listened, not hearing anything but my heart racing in my ears.

I turned my head around the couch just enough to see a dark shape lying on the floor, its back to me. Without stepping on any glass I slipped back out of the room, finding Desmond as he reentered the house through the mudroom. “Did you find anything?”

I waved him to follow me back to the living room, he was tall enough to see the person from the doorway. “It’s not your dad,” he signed, motioning for us to move upstairs. We kept our feet against the baseboards and the edge of the stairs to avoid the loose panels. Something crashed in my room and we froze.

I looked to Desmond to see him sliding up behind me, his long legs making it easy to maneuver around me so he could peak through the first door on the right. He turned to me and gestured his hands rapidly. “Injured, can’t see.” I asked him if it was my dad and he shook his head. I slid across the floor, hugging the far wall as best as I could to get to the other side of the door without being seen.

Something huge and dark pinned me to the wall, his gloved fingers gripped tightly around my neck. “Eve!” Desmond tackled us to the ground, the assailant’s hold on my windpipe knocked loose as the three of us tumbled down the stairs.

I rolled over the two as they grappled, landing in a large pile of broken figurines and splintered clock parts and narrowly avoiding the jagged pendulum when they rolled towards me. “Wait, Dad! It’s Desmond!”

Desmond disengaged, putting himself between me and the assailant. The man raised his head, the hard look in his eyes fading to fear. “What are you doing here? Why didn’t you go with your mom?” I slipped around to kneel in front of him. He had a large gash in his neck, just shy of slicing through enough for him to bleed out.
