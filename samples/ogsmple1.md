Dragon Made
2010

Ash opened her eyes, she must have been in one of her memories again. With a sigh she crawled out of the blankets and stretched, looking at the familiar soft green glow and listened to the song birds that drowned out the nearby stream. She looked up to see the ornamental mirror reflecting her in a white nightgown. The girl turned to make her bed, jumping when she saw her eggs glistening in the soft light that filtered through the window.

The girl frowned, the purple stone had shone up many times in her mast, but under any stage of creation had the white one had never followed her into her dreams, let alone her memories. “What are you doing here?” She muttered, taking them into her hands and sitting down with them in her lap.

“I was about to ask you the same thing Miss.” Came a gruff voice. She jump and look to the door to see an ancient, old man. She got up quickly and cradled the eggs to her chest like new born babies, this _wasn‘t_ a memory. “This tree house hasn’t had a soul in it since little Azarin had to run from the King and his dragon, and then suddenly there’s a young woman here with a chunk of amethyst and quartz in her arms.” He said, hobbling in on is walking stick. He squinted up at her, “What a pretty thing you are. How did you get here?”

“I-I’ve lived in this tree m-my whole life.” She stuttered, unable to keep control of herself. No living being besides the animals of the forest ventured this far into the old forests, too many dangers in coming in here. She remembered that much clearly.

Before he could reply, the sound of someone climbing the tree reached their ears. “Ash, Ash are you here?!”

“Benny?” She whispered, sidestepping the old man, placing herself by the pane-less window as she watched the old tattoo shop owner come in. “What’s going on, this isn’t normal.”

“I should say it’s not, what are you doing here Ron?” The old man said.

Panicking, she turned and jumped out the window; she wanted to get away from this nightmare.

Ron went into action as soon as he saw the girl try to escape out the window, catching her wrist and trying to pull her back. But when her head whipped around and he saw the alarm in her eyes that he had promised to banish, he let go. Ash slid down the tree trunk with catlike grace even when panicked and darted for the trees.

The two old men watched the girl run, losing her in the underbrush. “Ron, what is going on?” The older one asked.

Ron ran a hand through his hair, stress riddled across his face. “Azarin has come home; she has the dragon egg with her.”

The old man’s storm gray eyes sparkled, “Then it has begun.”

“It has begun.” He agreed solemnly, watching the spot where Ash had disappeared, hoping she would be alright.
