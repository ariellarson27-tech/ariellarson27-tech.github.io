Avatar the Last Airbender the Series
Fan-Fiction 2016

What’s so important about this person?  Ayala’s mind echoed with this thought over and over until they finally caught up with the Dragon of the West. He was a portly old man that seemed to know more about tea than even some of the monks that had cultivated rare herbs at the air temples, and yet he seemed haunted. “Lu, is it really safe to tell him who I am? What if he just reports me to the Fire Lord?”

Lu Ten’s face copied Iroh’s, seeming just as troubled as the man we had tracked down. “He’s… my father. And if Avatar Roku is right, then he should be trying to cross to the Spirit World to find me. And right now, the only person that can reunite us, even for just a moment, is you.” His eyes seemed to burn with passion as his gaze kept falling to the man, who was now sitting alone on the far side of a tea house at a Fire Nation settlement on the western coast of the Earth Kingdom Territory. “Once I can speak with him again, I can tell him how our conquest is upsetting the balance of not just this world, but the Spirit World as well. If he’s still the man that raised me, he will know what needs to be done, and will help you find your brother.”

They left the tea house before him, opting for camping out on the mountain side about the village. Ayala gazed down at where she could just make out an acting troupe playing out the tale of the Blue Spirit.

“So much has changed, how did it come to this?” Lu Ten usually commented when she asked questions like this, and she was happy the spirit seemed not to be present. “Probably still watching his father.”

The hair on the back of her neck stood on end with a static charge. Instinctively rolling to avoid the bolt of lightning, she landed in a crouch facing where the shot had been fired. “Your companion gives you away, young Avatar, Sky Bison went extinct during the Airbender Massacre.”

Iroh’s voice was rough with grief, the lines of age deepened with rage. “How dare you appear now, a hundred years later.” Ayala had to dive behind a boulder to avoid the next shot. “If it wasn’t for you, my son would never have died!” His roar echoed off the mountains like thunder, the next stream of lightning arching to get around the boulder.

The only way she could avoid it, was to redirect the lightning into the open air, matching the old general’s anguished cry. “I’m not your enemy!”

“Sing, sing it now!” Lu Ten called, trying his best to hold his father back.

_“Leaves from the vine… Falling so slow…_

_“Like fagile, tiny shells… Drifting in the foam…_

Iroh fell still, staring at her in disbelief. His eyes filled with tears, and he fell to his knees as Ayala’s singing came to an end. “Not even my nephew knows that song…” His body shook with a ragged sigh.

Lu Ten knelt in front of him, placing his hand firmly on his father’s shoulder. “It’s the only way I could think of getting you to listen, Father.”

He looked up to her in astonishment, hope lighting his eyes. “My son, he’s here?” He placed his hand on his shoulder, passing through Lu Ten’s to clutch at his clothes.

“He’s the one who helped me find you…” Ayala said, “Would you like to see him?” She held her hand out to him invitingly. His trembling fingers gripped hers tightly, so much hope filling the man compared to what she had seen in the tea shop. She closed her eyes, letting the strength of her spirit flow through him enough to see what she could see.

When Iroh opened his eyes, the girl had disappeared, but slowly through the dark, his silhouette appeared. “Oh my boy, my wonderful boy.” He sobbed, taking the young man into his arms. To feel him hugging him again, made him ache knowing it would be the last time. “I’m so sorry…”

Lu Ten squeezed him comfortingly, “It’s alright Father, you taught me well. I took a boulder for one of my soldiers, giving them enough time to retreat. We were ambushed.” He sighed, pulling back to arm’s length to better see his father’s face. “But that’s not important. Father, I know about the Order of the White Lotus. They are _right._ If the Avatar is to succeed, he will need their help… _your_ help Father. Without it, he will die before the coming of Sozen’s Comet.”

Iroh stared, gaping like a fish. “But, you don’t even _like_ Pai Shou!”

Lu Ten chuckled, “I don’t have to like playing it to appreciate the game. But Father, if the Fire Naiton is not stopped, if your brother accomplishes this conquest. The worlds will forever be out of balance, and even the Avatar won’t be able to bring it back.”

“This girl is supposed to go against your uncle? I wouldn’t ever dream of going against him! Between him and my father, sitting in a room with them would ruin even the best batch of tea. Fighting them would be inviting death.” Iroh shook his head, “She wouldn’t be able to defeat him.”

“Ayala isn’t the Avatar father, her brother is. And he’s lost, suspended in time as she was.” He gestured to where the girl lay unconscious behind him. “She doesn’t understand this world father, she’s still just a child, no older than Zuko. If this world is to return to balance, she needs your help to save her brother so he can save us all.” He kissed Iroh on the forehead, “I love you Father, stay strong.”

Iroh gripped at his son helplessly as he began to fade away into the darkness, the last thing he said still echoing in his ears. _Protect her father, like the daughter you never had._
