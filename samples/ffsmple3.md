Hawksong by Amelia Atwater-Rhodes
Fan-Fiction 2014

I woke with a start, screaming because the last thing I’d seen was a big knife coming near my throat. I wiped my face with both hands, holding my breath as my heartbeat rushed in my ears. It’d felt so _real_. I’d barely gotten myself calmed down when my mom was flying into the room. “I heard you screaming, is everything alright?”

I looked at the blue light glowing on my bedside table, it read half past two in the morning. “Just a nightmare, Mom, I’m fine.” But I could still see the dagger coming at me, could still see the woman’s unreadable eyes burning into her. I smiled, “Go back to bed, I’ll be fine.”

She smiled sleepily, closing the door again, leaving me in the dark. I began to shake uncontrollably. Turning on my bedside light I pulled my legs up to my chest, trying to calm down. Flashes of other nightmares flitted vividly through my head. Before I could calm down I was thrown into one.

Something had hit me while I was flying. I tumbled through the air, crashing through trees as I tried to slow my fall. I’d barely been able to grab a branch when I heard shouting lower in the trees. I was too scared to call out. Scrabbling onto the thick limb, I limped to the trunk and clung to it, searching the ground for the people the voices belonged to.

A trio of people in what looked like light armor exploded from the underbrush, I could see them clearly. Two of them had dark hair, almost black. The first had black hair, the sheen of his hair the color of an oil slick. My heart quickened, I wanted to run, to fly. I looked to my wing. The broken shaft of an arrow was keeping it from healing. I gripped the wood with my beak and slowly pulled it out.

The arrow slipped, falling back to the ground, landing at the black-haired man’s feet. Two deep red eyes stared at me, freezing me in place. _Danger!_ My instincts screamed, _fly!_ I didn’t move, I couldn’t move.

I woke again, in a cold sweat. Gasping and clutching my sides I fell out of bed. It was five in the morning now and my alarm clock was ringing. The blaring was almost shaking the clock by the time I managed to hit snooze.

The clock read seven-thirty, “Damn it!” I was late for school, _again._

It was rainy, slippery, plain-old yucky the entire way to school, making the start to my day that much worse by the time I slid into my English class. Since we were studying Emily Dickinson I had to recite _Much Madness is divinest Sense._ I’d gotten to the last verse when he interrupted and told off the rest of the class for not memorizing their lines over the break. He winked, and the day went on.

I didn’t notice something was wrong until third. That’s when I noticed the raven looming on the building as I entered the re-locatable for psychology. It wasn’t weird for them and crows to be around the school during lunch, they were almost as bad as seagulls. But none of them swooped at you after you noticed them.

“It almost hit her!” “I can’t believe it did that.” “Don’t tell me the bird freak has a pet crow, that’s just crazy.” Ignoring the class I slipped into my seat, pulling out my notes to study the test. When the period was over I checked the sky for the bird. There was no sign of it. The rest of the school day ended without another glimpse of it.
